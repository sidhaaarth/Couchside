## (a) Project description

**Couchside: your phone's lock screen becomes the Fire TV remote, and it can catch up anyone who joined late.**

**What it does**

Couchside pairs a Fire TV app with an Android phone app.

- **Lock-screen remote.** Whatever is playing on the TV appears on the phone's lock screen and in the notification shade, like a music player. You get the title, a scrubber that moves in real time, skip back or forward 15 seconds, and play/pause, all without unlocking the phone or picking up the TV remote.
- **"What did they say?"** A custom button on that same lock-screen card. It rewinds the TV 10 seconds and turns subtitles on for 12 seconds, so you can catch a line you missed. Then subtitles turn off again. The TV shows a small `↺ 10s · subtitles on` badge while this is happening.
- **"Catch me up."** When someone sits down mid-film, they tap Catch me up on the phone. The TV takes the subtitle dialogue from 0:00 up to the current playback position, and nothing past it, so the recap can't spoil anything ahead. An AWS Lambda function sends that dialogue to Amazon Bedrock (Amazon Nova 2 Lite, with Nova Micro as a fallback), and a short prose recap appears on the phone, usually in 2–4 seconds. The recap gets longer the further into the film you are: about 40 words near the start, rising to 150 by the end. 150 words is what fits on one phone screen.

**How it works**

- The **Fire TV app** (Kotlin, Media3 ExoPlayer, minSdk 28 / Fire OS 7) streams *Charade* (1963, public domain in the US) from archive.org. It loads an SRT subtitle file alongside the video; we generated that file from archive.org's auto-captions. It runs a small TCP server on port 8765 and sends its playback state (title, position, duration, playing) every 500 ms as newline-delimited JSON. It accepts commands: play, pause, toggle, seek, back, fwd, whatSay, catchUp.
- The **phone app** (Kotlin, Media3 session) runs a `MediaSessionService` whose player is a `SimpleBasePlayer` that mirrors the TV's state and forwards every control back over the socket. Because it is a real media session, Android draws the lock-screen and notification controls for us, including the custom "What did they say?" button. The app itself is a Fire TV-style companion: a home screen with a now-playing banner and a Now Playing screen. Catch me up switches it to a lyrics-style view: the recap on a grey rounded tile, with the scrubber and controls kept at the bottom.
- For **Catch me up**, the TV cuts the transcript at the current position and POSTs it, together with the position and duration, to a **Lambda Function URL**. The Lambda function (Python 3.13, boto3) works out how much of the film has been watched, sets a word target (40 + 110 × share), and calls the **Bedrock Converse API**. If the first model fails it tries the next one, and it trims the answer at the last complete sentence. No AWS credentials are stored on the TV or the phone.

Built with: Kotlin, AndroidX Media3 (ExoPlayer, Session), Fire OS, Amazon Bedrock (Amazon Nova), AWS Lambda, IAM, CloudWatch Logs, Python.

Sample content: *Charade* (Universal Pictures, 1963), public domain in the US. The copy comes from archive.org item batvwi-Charade_1963. Fire TV branding assets were supplied for the demo.

## (b) Tracks and mini challenges

- **Track: Fire TV** (primary)
- **Mini challenge: AWS Builder**
- **Priority categories addressed:**
  - **Multi-modal UX.** Phone lock screen, notification shade and companion app work together with the TV screen. Controls and recaps live on the phone, and the TV shows on-screen feedback (badges, subtitles).
  - **AI-enhanced viewing.** Catch me up gives a spoiler-safe recap generated by Amazon Nova on Bedrock, using only dialogue up to the current position.
- **Platform: Fire OS.** The TV app is a native Android (Kotlin) app built for Fire OS 7 and 8 (`minSdk 28`), using only standard Android APIs and no Google services. It was developed and demoed on the Android TV emulator (Android 11, the base of Fire OS 8), since Amazon offers no Fire OS simulator; the official simulator, the Vega Virtual Device, runs Vega OS apps only. This is the same Fire OS workflow as Amazon's own [react-native-multi-tv-app-sample](https://github.com/AmazonAppDev/react-native-multi-tv-app-sample), which builds and runs its "Android TV and Fire TV FOS" target with the Android SDK.

## (c) AWS Builder: how AWS is integrated

```
Fire TV app ──HTTPS POST {title, position, duration, transcript-so-far}──► Lambda Function URL
                                                                     │
                                                       Lambda "couch-recap" (Python 3.13)
                                                                     │  bedrock-runtime Converse
                                                                     ▼
                                                  Amazon Bedrock: us.amazon.nova-2-lite-v1:0
                                                       (fallback us.amazon.nova-micro-v1:0)
                                                                     │
                                         {"recap": "..."} ◄──────────┘   logs → CloudWatch Logs
```

| Service | How it's used |
|---|---|
| **Amazon Bedrock (Converse API)** | `bedrock-runtime.converse()` The system prompt gives the film title, the current position, and the share watched with a word target ("The viewer is N% of the way through, so aim for about T words: the further in, the fuller the catch-up should be"). It also says to use only this dialogue and not to speculate about what happens next. The user message is the dialogue so far. `maxTokens` = limit × 2 + 60. Models are tried in order from the `MODEL_ID` env var: **Amazon Nova 2 Lite** first, **Amazon Nova Micro** as fallback, both called through `us.*` cross-region inference profiles in us-east-2. Because the Converse API is the same for every model, switching models is only a config change. |
| **AWS Lambda** | Function `couch-recap` (python3.13, 30 s, 256 MB), boto3 only, no extra dependencies. It validates input (JSON object, transcript present, ≤ 200,000 characters; a full 2-hour film's transcript is about 62–75k). It computes share = position ÷ duration (capped at 1; a 120-min film is assumed if the duration is missing), target = round(40 + 110 × share) words and limit = min(150, target + 25). Then it calls Bedrock and trims the reply to the last complete sentence within the limit. Shared-secret check: requests must carry `x-recap-token` matching the `RECAP_TOKEN` env var (kept locally in a git-ignored `.env`), otherwise 401. |
| **Lambda Function URL** | Gives the TV app an HTTPS endpoint without API Gateway (AuthType NONE for the demo). The TV stores no AWS credentials. |
| **IAM** | Role `couch-recap-role`: `AWSLambdaBasicExecutionRole` plus an inline policy allowing `bedrock:InvokeModel` / `InvokeModelWithResponseStream`. |
| **Amazon CloudWatch Logs** | The Lambda function logs which model answered, the word count and the limit, and any per-model Bedrock errors. We used these logs to compare Nova models and debug fallback. |

Deployment is one script, `recap/deploy.ps1`, which uses the AWS CLI and is safe to re-run. The handler has 9 unit tests that use a fake Bedrock client.

Measured live with Nova 2 Lite, using the full *Charade* dialogue up to each point (the film is about 117 min):

| Position | Share watched | Recap length | Latency |
|---|---|---|---|
| 18:00 | 15% | 69 words | 4.2 s |
| 47:00 | 40% | 109 words | 1.7 s |
| 82:00 | 70% | 106 words | 2.6 s |
| 115:00 | 98% | 119 words | 3.2 s |

End to end through the TV app at 47:00: 1.9 s.

## (d) Product feedback: DRAFT (rewrite in your own words)

### Amazon Devices Builder Tools (MCP server + skills)
- **What I used it for:** I set it up with `npx @amazon-devices/amazon-devices-buildertools-mcp init-context` and used it to look up Fire TV/Vega documentation (`search_documentation`, `list_documents`, `read_document`) while building the Fire OS app.
- **What worked:** Once configured, doc search was fast and the right pages came up. The tool set (list_documents, search_documentation, read_document, read_asset, set_project_context, analyze_perfetto_traces, get_app_hot_functions, symbolicate_acr, report_workflow_status) covers a lot.
- **What needs work:** The docs say "full support for Vega OS and limited support for Fire OS", and most skills are Vega-only, so it helped less with a Fire OS Kotlin app. Better Fire OS / Media3 coverage would help.
- **Onboarding:** `init-context` for Claude Code failed with "Claude Code not installed". It checks for `claude` on the PATH (`where claude`), but the CLI bundled with the VS Code extension isn't on the PATH. When run from the home directory it wrote `CLAUDE.md` there, which then applied to every project. The project-identifier prompt was confusing.
- **Would I build with it again?** [your call]

### Amazon Bedrock
- **What I used it for:** Generating the Catch me up recaps through the Converse API.
- **What worked:** The Amazon Nova models (Micro, Lite, 2 Lite, Pro) worked right away through the Converse API with `us.*` inference profiles. Nova 2 Lite gave noticeably better recaps than Nova Micro: it got who's who right and followed the format. The Converse API made switching models a one-line change.
- **What needs work:** Claude 5.x models (Haiku/Sonnet/Opus 5.5) were listed as ACTIVE in `list-foundation-models` but calls returned "not available for this account". Claude Haiku 4.5 then needed the Anthropic use-case form plus an AWS Marketplace subscription, which requires `aws-marketplace:Subscribe` on the first caller. That was a confusing multi-step process, and the error messages changed over about 15 minutes. Titan Text Lite/Express returned "reached the end of its life", but they are still listed.
- **Onboarding:** Nova was available immediately. Third-party models took several steps that weren't obvious from the errors.
- **Would I build with it again?** [your call]

### AWS Lambda (Function URLs)
- **What I used it for:** Hosting the recap endpoint the TV calls.
- **What worked:** It was the easiest way to give a TV app an HTTPS endpoint, with no API Gateway and no credentials on the device.
- **What needs work:** [optional: e.g., setting up the public-URL permissions took two `add-permission` calls on newer accounts]
- **Would I build with it again?** [your call]

### AndroidX Media3 (ExoPlayer + Session)
- **What I used it for:** Video and subtitle playback on the TV, plus lock-screen controls on the phone.
- **What worked:** `SimpleBasePlayer` + `MediaSessionService` gave me system lock-screen controls, including custom buttons (`setMediaButtonPreferences`, `ICON_SKIP_BACK_15`), with very little code. Turning subtitles on and off is one track-selection parameter.
- **What needs work:** [your call]
- **Would I build with it again?** [your call]

### Fire TV development without hardware
- I used the Android TV emulator as a stand-in for Fire OS. It works for development, but there's no official Fire OS emulator, so testing Fire OS-specific behavior without a device is hard.
